One melted candy bar helped spark the microwave oven.
In nineteen forty-five, engineer Percy Spencer was working with radar equipment when he noticed the chocolate in his pocket had softened.
He tested the strange effect with popcorn kernels. They popped.
Then he tried an egg. It burst.
Spencer realized the equipment’s invisible energy could cook food.
His team developed the idea into an oven—but the first model stood six feet tall and weighed more than seven hundred fifty pounds.
A pocket-sized accident led to a kitchen giant.
